## Why

The 16 alarms of `forma-beta-trial-alarms` watch AWS parts (5xx counts, CPU, memory, database storage), not what a person experiences. Nothing probes the beta or fAuth from outside AWS. There is no error *rate*, no latency signal and no single screen to look at during the class. These are the "golden signals" (traffic, errors, latency, saturation) a mature SaaS team watches first, and every one of them comes from metrics AWS already has.

## What changes

1. `ops/monitoring/observability.json`, a new standalone stack `forma-beta-observability` reusing the alarm topic:
   - Two Route 53 HTTPS health checks (beta `/health/ready`, fAuth `/health`), each from three regions, every 30 s, with an alarm per check ("app unreachable", "fAuth unreachable").
   - An error-rate SLO alarm: 5xx ÷ requests > 2 % for 2 of 3 five-minute periods, counted only when there are at least 50 requests.
   - A median-latency alarm: ALB p50 > 2 s for 15 minutes. The p95 is legitimately slow on model calls.
   - The `forma-beta-golden-signals` dashboard: traffic and WAF, errors, latency p50/p90/p99, saturation (ECS, RDS, fAuth host), health, and email.
2. **AWS Cost Anomaly Detection** (free): one service monitor and a daily email subscription to victor@getforma.dev, as a small stack or by console. It complements the fixed budget with "unusual for us" alerts.
3. Runbook §11.8: what each alarm means, the first check, deploy, roll back and cost.

## Verification

## Migration and rollback

`aws cloudformation delete-stack --stack-name forma-beta-observability` removes everything; nothing else depends on it.
