## Why

Reviewers will not accept a canvas that already holds apps and records. The link has to admit one new member into an empty organization, show an empty canvas, and carry one real workflow: describe an app, add one record, share it. That member is the only person on the canvas. The recipient does not already have an account. Accepting the share is what makes them sign up and sign in. They arrive with no other apps, and the shared app is the one both members work from. A stranger gets the uniform 404. The entry for the first member is an invite, not a public signup. The recorded video of this path cannot run past three minutes. Someone using the link may take longer. The link is not cut to fit the recording.

## What changes

The later [ADR-014](../../docs/architecture/ADR-014-LIVE-APP-SHARING.md) and APP-154 supersede the original membership assumption below: the recipient becomes a verified app guest, not a Northstar member. APP-154 owns the share authority, identity-only arrival and connected canvas implementation; this ticket qualifies that path in the complete trial.

Verify the first authoring transition implemented by APP-145. `Workspace::composer_context` stays read-only; intentional compose uses `prepare_composer`, selects an eligible owned app or creates the first shell, and retains the existing publication-policy requirement (`app/src/storage/composer.rs`). The clean dev stand still pre-creates a shell (`app/src/dev/db.rs`), so this trial must instead reach first compose through a real invitation/sign-in with no pre-created app. APP-145 owns that grant transaction; this ticket does not duplicate it. No server-side layout store or second demo mode.

Then a short guide on the empty canvas names the next moves. It does not read `GENUI_DEMO_MODE` and it does not use a rehearsed sentence. The app appears through compose and admission. The record is the existing command path. The share has to reach someone who is not a member yet. Today's `share_work` grants only an active member of the same organization and refuses anyone else (`app/src/storage/sharing.rs`). The slice that follows the authoring shell lets the share be the reason that person creates an account. After they sign in they hold this app and no other. No new permission model beyond that acceptance, no server-side layout store, and no second demo mode.

Provider invitation delivery is another prerequisite, not just a share-row change. The current operator writes the capability secret and `app/runtime/runtime.py` materializes it only at the next service task start; `app/runtime/admission_operator.py` documents the same boundary. A newly shared recipient therefore needs a reviewed server-side invitation creation/delivery path that becomes usable while the service stays running. Reuse verified provider admission and bounded issuance/revocation; do not send provider capabilities to the browser or grant the ordinary workspace broad operator authority. Preserve the intended recipient and exact work through provider verification and callback.

The seeded two-window rehearsal stays in APP-096, APP-098, APP-105, and APP-110. It is not this trial.

## Acceptance tests

- [ ] One invite admits a new member into an organization with no library apps, no placed cards, and no records. The canvas opens empty.
- [ ] That member can describe one app and see it appear through the existing composer and admission path, including when they owned no app at sign-in.
- [ ] That member adds one record through the existing command path. A shown number carries its receipt. Nothing uses `GENUI_DEMO_MODE` or a rehearsed sentence.
- [ ] The first member is the only person on the canvas. They share the work with someone who does not already have an account. Accepting that share is what makes the recipient sign up and sign in. The recipient arrives with no other apps, and both members work from the shared app. A stranger gets the uniform 404.
- [ ] A sanitized click log of the try-it link exists, and a recording of the same path stays within three minutes. Neither contains a credential, invite token, or model key. The link is not required to finish in three minutes.

- [ ] The pending share binds the organization, saved work, intended verified recipient and bounded permissions. An expired/revoked/replayed invite, wrong recipient or revoked sharer cannot grant access; retrying acceptance grants once. Share context survives signup without becoming a bearer grant to the work. Acceptance resumes the exact saved work and does not expose sibling works that share the same authoring app. Independently review this authority change (Tier A).
- [ ] The recipient opens the authorized app page and adds the same saved work to their own canvas. A committed owner change reaches the open recipient view through reauthorized live updates; record measured latency and reconnect recovery. State explicitly whether the share is view-only or also permits records to be written.

- [ ] A recipient invited by an authorized in-product share can complete actual provider signup without an operator creating the invite by hand or restarting/redeploying the service. Capability delivery, failure recovery and revoke/expiry remain bounded and independently reviewed; no provider invitation secret enters browser state or logs.

## Verification

Read `choose_authoring` and the invite path before changing publish. The first slice is one branch and one fresh-browser check on a worktree port. Ports 4001–4003 and 4010 stay untouched. Review depth for the authoring shell is Tier A (ADR-010 §4): it grants `edit_definition` and `run` to the new member on an app they own.

## Migration and rollback

The authoring-shell schema and catalog change belong to APP-145 (migration 0016 in PR 25). Share-to-signup needs a reviewed durable binding between invitation and saved work; determine and record its migration before implementation rather than assuming no migration. Existing active-member sharing remains the grant boundary. The guide is removable canvas copy. Do not roll back a migrated service to a binary that refuses the new catalog.
