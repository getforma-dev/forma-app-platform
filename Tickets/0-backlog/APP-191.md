## Why

A general platform needs lists whose entries are private to their creator: responses, submissions, claims, applications, tickets. The app's reviewers see and act on all of them, and only reviewers may approve, grade or close. Today anyone who can read a work sees every record, and anyone who can write can run every command.

## What changes

A record schema gains an optional `visibility: "own"` (absent means shared) and a command gains an optional `reviewer_only: true`, under a contract-edition update. Each meaning has one encoding, and older definitions stay valid. A work permission `record_review` is added. One enforcement rule covers every read and write path: lists, queries, summaries, components, canvas, ledger, guests, commands, live events, Ask, MCP, Slack, receipts and exports. The composer can produce both properties from plain words, with no classroom-named templates. The design is picked from two independent sketches before building.

## Acceptance tests

- [ ] In an `own` list, a contributor sees and changes only their own entries, and summaries count only their entries, on every path. A reviewer or the app owner sees and changes all of them. A campaign walks every path with a contributor, a reviewer, another member and a guest.
- [ ] A reviewer-only command is refused for contributors, even on their own entries, and allowed for reviewers and the owner.
- [ ] Each path's check fails with the enforcement bypassed. Existing definitions and the data, folder, sharing and pitch campaigns are unchanged.
- [ ] An independent Tier A review and recheck find no blocker.

## Verification

The new visibility campaign, the existing campaigns, admission and contract tests, and the review in `evidence/APP-191/`.

## Migration and rollback
