## Why

Victor, 2026-10-30: "modals are awesome for focus work … a [] button … brings their work into focus and they can switch too when in modal view. Then back to canvas for the whole picture. Ohh and maybe a unite view like the power rangers when they unite. So the whole app assembles?"

The canvas is good for the whole picture. Focus mode serves actual work: filling the expense form, reading a long table, deciding three claims. Unite turns a scattered set of cards back into the app they came from. On stage it makes "cards are pieces of one governed app" visible in two seconds.

## What changes

1. **Focus mode.**
   - Every card's header gets a **focus** button (`[ ]` icon, tooltip "Focus · F"). Pressing **F** on a selected card does the same.
   - The card opens in a centered modal sized for work (about 80% of the viewport, full-screen sheet at phone width) over a dimmed, blurred canvas. Its content is the same live card at a larger size: forms usable, tables scrollable, receipts visible.
   - In the modal, **← / →** (and swipe) move to the previous or next card of the same app, or through the stack the card came from. A strip of small thumbnails at the bottom shows where you are and jumps on click. **Esc**, the close button or a click on the backdrop returns to the canvas with the card where it was.
   - Live updates keep flowing in focus. A pending approval that arrives shows a badge on its thumbnail.
   - The deep link is `#focus=<card id>`, so a refresh reopens the same card.
2. **Unite (assemble).**
   - On any app (an app header or tile menu, the library pane's app row, `/unite <app>`, or **U** with an app's card selected), its cards animate together into the app's own composed layout: one frame with the header, the KPI numbers in a row, the main chart, the table, and the form or records beside them.
   - The motion is a staggered fly-in, about 600–800 ms, eased. Cards keep their identity through the animation (no remount, so receipts and form state survive), then settle into a single bordered "app frame" titled with the app name and its retrieved, adapted or generated label.
   - Cards of the app that are not yet on the canvas are brought in for the assembly.
   - **Break apart** (the frame menu, `/split <app>`, or **Shift+U**) reverses the motion and returns each card to its previous canvas position.
   - A united app behaves like a stack in the layout: it moves as one and persists per member (DS-06/DS-67). Focus mode works inside it.
   - The layout is computed from the app's definition (its views and commands, in declared order) with a fixed set of arrangements: KPI row, primary visual, detail table, side panel. No model is involved.
3. **Discoverability.**
   - The `?` sheet lists F, U, Shift+U, ← / → and Esc.
   - The canvas toolbar menu gets "Focus selected" and "Unite app".
   - `docs/pitch/GUIDE.md` and `SCENARIOS.md` gain one line each. The pitch's expense beat ends with "Unite" so the room sees the whole expense app assemble.

## Acceptance tests

- [ ] F or the focus button opens the selected card in the modal. ← / → walk the app's cards (or the stack). Esc returns to the canvas with no layout change.
- [ ] A form filled in focus mode submits through the existing audited command route, and the canvas card shows the result after closing.
- [ ] A live event (for example Sarah approving) updates the focused card or its thumbnail while the modal is open.
- [ ] `/unite expense` assembles all of Expense claims' cards into one frame in under 1 s with the motion. Break apart restores every card's previous position. Both survive a reload.
- [ ] Receipts, form drafts and scroll positions survive focus, unite and break apart (no remount).
- [ ] Keyboard shortcuts never fire while typing. Focus is trapped in the modal and returned to the card on close (accessibility). `prefers-reduced-motion` shortens unite to a cross-fade.
- [ ] Phone width: focus is a full-screen sheet with swipe between cards, and unite stacks the frame in a single column.
