## Why

Today deletion is organization-wide (`purge-organization`, APP-212) or partial (`/admin` Remove, `revoke-membership`, `delete-uploads`). Keeping everyone by default means deletion becomes per member, on request, and must be complete and provable. The plan's §2.3 inventories a member's data table by table. The binding constraint is `internal_record_revisions.creator_id/updater_id → genui.principals` (`app/migrations/0002_tenant_foundation.sql:168-178`): a member who entered records in another owner's app cannot have their principal row deleted without deleting that owner's records, so the principal row stays as an identifier and the name goes.

## What changes

1. **Migration 0026 (with APP-229):** the operator kind `member.erased`.
2. **`admission erase-member --organization <org> --principal <id> --confirm <id>`** in `admission_operator.py`, one transaction as `genui_owner`, following `purge_organization`'s shape (counts before, refuse-and-roll-back proofs, counts after): requires the membership already `revoked` (refuses `member_active`), refuses the last owner (`last_owner`) and an unknown principal. Then: revoke entitlements; delete sessions and pre-access sessions, agent attributions and tokens, Slack links, member cards and labels, their `box_files`/boxes/grants/briefs rows (after the S3 sweep), the apps they own with everything under them (children before parents, the purge's walk restricted to `owner_principal_id`), shares they made or sponsor and shares made to them (a guest's `share_events` stay, as at the purge); set `display_name` NULL and role `member`; keep the principal, membership, consent, redemption and audit rows. **Records they created or updated in other owners' apps:** per Victor's Q3 answer, either untouched (default; the principal row is their anonymous author) or their revisions deleted and the record's current version repointed. Afterwards prove no content row names the member (`erase_incomplete`) and that the five audit chains did not move (`erase_audit_changed`), append one `member.erased` event, and return the counts.
3. **`ops/promotion/admission.py erase-member`:** runs `delete-uploads` first (exists; refuses more than 100 files), then the task, writes the intent and result receipts, and prints the `next` step: the fAuth deletion.
4. **The fAuth step** (runbook §10 step 6, per user): `POST /manage/users/{id}/revoke-sessions`, `DELETE /manage/users/{id}`, the `stats` before and after, recorded with the receipt; optionally the `tenant_user_invites` and `login_attempts` address rows by SQL on the fAuth database.
5. **Runbook:** a "Deletion on request" section replacing the purge as the standing procedure: the privacy@ intake, the 7-day confirmation, the steps above in order, the reply template naming what stayed, and the 30-day deadline; §10's purge commands remain documented as a tool. The notice v2 sentence on entries in others' apps must match the Q3 choice.
6. **Not here:** a self-service "delete my account" button (later, with the tiers), and the member-side export.

## Migration and rollback

The kind is APP-229's migration 0026. An erase is irreversible by design once committed; its receipts and the fAuth log are the record. Before the task commits, every refusal rolls back and deletes nothing; the S3 sweep runs first and is the only step without a transaction, as at the purge.
