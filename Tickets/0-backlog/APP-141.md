## Why

The canvas currently shows an app card and other cards that often do not read as one thing. APP-120 already settled the objects: an app is the only governed thing and it contains cards; a widget is a one-off (weather, calculator, clock, note, folder); a stack, a unite frame, and focus are arrangements and are not listed in the library. What is missing is the moment the app is created. Victor wants that moment to happen before anything is dropped on the board, and he wants it to be explicit.

## What exists today

- The library pane lists admitted apps the member may open (`GET /v1/library`). Adding one places its cards on the board. That is placement, not creation.
- Describing something new goes through `composer::routes::compose_for` and `Composer::generate_with`. The reply is parsed, pinned to the member's sources, admitted whole, checked with `require_authored`, and published onto an app the member already owns (`choose_authoring` in `app/src/storage/composer.rs`).
- A member who owns no app gets `no_authoring_app`. The clean dev stand hides this by running `create-app` first (`app/src/dev/db.rs`). An invitation does not. That hole is APP-139. This ticket does not close it, and the description step cannot succeed for a brand-new member until APP-139 does.
- The library catalog has no industry taxonomy. Fields named `category` inside a fixture are record fields, not a menu of industries.

## What changes

1. From an empty canvas, the member chooses Create an app. A modal opens. The board does not gain a card.
2. The modal has three lists the member can switch: Industries, Categories, and Favorites. Industries and categories are a fixed grouping of the admitted templates, written by us, enough for the common cases. Favorites are templates this member has marked. They are a preference for this member, not a grant and not a tenant-wide setting.
3. The member picks one template. The modal then asks for the description. That sentence is the second step, not the first. Cancel, or closing the modal, places nothing and calls no model.
4. Confirm sends the description through the existing composer. The model returns data. It does not return HTML, script, SQL, FMIR, or a grant. Admission can refuse it. A refusal leaves the canvas empty and shows the refusal code. It does not place a partial app.
5. On success there is one app. Its cards are the parts the admitted definition declared: the views and the form. They are addressed the way APP-120 already addresses them, `app.card` or the pane number. The member then sees that app. Putting it on the board is the confirm, not a separate drag.

A widget is not created here. Weather, calculator, clock, note, and folder stay widgets. Placing one does not create an app.

A fully custom app, with no template, is later. This slice does not offer a blank definition.

## What this ticket does not decide

These stay open. Do not build them under this ticket.

- A card leaving the app and becoming a widget while its data stays.
- A card removed from the app whose records are kept.
- Whether the folder (the box in APP-108) stays. APP-120 already says a folder is a widget and its formation produces an app. Do not remove folders while that question is open.

## Acceptance tests

- [ ] From an empty canvas the member opens a modal, picks an industry or a category or a favorite, then one template, and no card is on the board yet.
- [ ] The description is the step after the template. Confirm sends it through `compose_for`. Cancel sends nothing. A refusal leaves the canvas empty.
- [ ] Success is one app. Its cards are the admitted views and form, addressed as APP-120 already addresses them. A custom-from-scratch app is not offered.
- [ ] A calculator, clock, or weather widget placed beside that app is still a widget and has no cards.
- [ ] A member who owns no authoring app still gets `no_authoring_app` until APP-145. This ticket does not hide that refusal.

## Verification

Read APP-120 and `choose_authoring` before building. A new member who owns no app is APP-139's hole. This ticket does not invent a second permission model. One fresh-browser check on a worktree port. Ports 4001–4003 and 4010 stay untouched.

## Migration and rollback

No migration. Removing the modal returns creation to today's composer and library placement. Apps already created stay.
