## Why

Sharing proves the permission model is real: the teammate sees the app only under the grants the teammate holds after the share, which are the sharer's own permissions on that work, **copied** (ADR-011 amendment 2026-10-30, review M3): nothing the sharer did not hold, nothing the definitions do not carry. `source_read` is copied too (the teammate needs it to open the work) and is organization-wide by the 0002 model, so it outlives the share.

## What changes

Publish or share a composed app within the organization under the existing owner-publish policy. A teammate opens it with the grants the share copied from the sharer (and any it already held) and sees only the data those grants allow; a non-member requesting the app, its modules or its data gets the uniform 404. Every share that wrote a row is recorded (`work_shares`: sharer, grantee, exactly the inserted permissions, the audit event), so it can be reversed row by row; an unshare route is not built (APP-097 debt).

## Acceptance tests

- [ ] The owner shares an app; the teammate opens it and sees data only through the teammate's own grants.
- [ ] A non-member and an unauthenticated caller receive the uniform 404, identical for existing and nonexistent apps.
- [ ] Revoking the share removes access at the next request.
- [ ] A shared app contains only admitted definitions; sharing grants no access the definitions do not carry.

## Verification

Two verified identities in the clone plus one outsider; retain statuses and sanitized screenshots.

## Migration and rollback

Sharing is additive; unsharing restores the owner-only state.
