## 2. Findings

### Major

1. **R6, appendix D, pages 59 to 61.** The 21 rows TH8 to TH28 drop the invariant at stake, the existing-control column and the response column, and state the threat as a clause rather than the grammar 10.4 uses ("A [source] with [prerequisites] can [action] which leads to [impact], negatively impacting [assets]"). TH8 to TH11 are High-impact rows and the attack tree in 10.4 hangs on TH8, TH9, TH10, TH12, TH17, TH18, TH19, TH20, TH27; a reader who follows a tree leaf into the appendix finds no invariant to check the mitigation against. Fix: add an Invariant column (one SI id per row, about 60 words in total), an Existing control column ("none" where true, which is most rows; the nonce CSP in TH25 and the prototype cap are the exceptions), and make the response explicit per row; rewrite the eleven rows named above in the full grammar; the rest may stay as clauses if the head accepts it.

2. **R5, R18, GUIDE section 4; pages 24, 26, 29 (and 14, 45).** The three DFD pages print their arrow labels at about 1.3 mm line height, roughly 4 pt on A4 landscape; the 4x crops show the labels only as shapes. Page 29 (flow 3) occupies the upper half of the page and leaves the lower half empty, so it is small for no reason. The container and deployment diagrams (14, 45) are at about 5 to 6 pt, borderline. Node names and titles are legible on all seven; the sequence diagram (31) is legible. The record's own rule is that "the rendered pages are the final judge" (M19). Fix: re-render the three DFDs with a larger label font or split flow 1 (21 arrows) into two diagrams (import and parse; compose, admit, publish); scale flow 3 to the page; raise the label font on 02 and 08 one step.

3. **R17, the whole record.** 48 body pages against 40 (20% over), with sections 6, 10 and 11 the heaviest. The density is also the readability problem of finding 6, so a length pass helps both. Fix: move to the appendix the demo-reductions table (11.1), the migration list (11.1, keeping one sentence), the secrets table and the security-event table (10.6, keeping the fail-closed paragraph), and the cost table's unit-price column; cut 6.6 to the hard rows; the component cards can lose their second example sentence each.

4. **R2, R3; 4.2 N2 against 5.2 "Availability (N2)".** N2 says the 99.5% objective holds "under one full restore at RTO (A7)"; the fit check says "one full restore at RTO, 4 hours, exceeds that by 21 minutes, so the objective assumes no full restore in a month." Both cannot be true; a reader takes the objective one way from section 4 and the other from section 5. Fix: pick one. Either N2 reads "excluding a full restore, which by itself spends the month's budget" or the objective drops to 99.4% (4.38 hours) to cover one restore; 11.3 SLO1 follows.

5. **Rendering, pages 1, 37, 38, 39, 59 to 61.** Table ID columns are too narrow for their tokens: the cover's metadata labels break mid-word ("Auth / or", "Statu / s", "Revi / ewer / s", "Appr / over / s"); on page 39 the STRIDE table, which is core reading, prints "TH1" as "T / H / 1", the STRIDE column breaks "Information disclosure" into "Inform / ation / disclos / ure", the Invariant header runs vertically one letter a line and "SI1" becomes "SI / 1"; pages 37 and 38 print "AS1" as "A / S1", "AG2" as "AG / 2", "TA10" as "TA1 / 0"; page 59 prints TH8 as "T / H / 8". The reader can decode it, but the one table the core points into is the worst case. Fix: a minimum width for ID columns (the IDs are at most four characters) and `nowrap` on them; render 10.4 and appendix D in landscape or drop the separate Invariant and STRIDE columns into the statement cell as a prefix ("[SI1; I, E]").

6. **R15, GUIDE 3 section 2 (the newcomer test); sections 2, 3, 5, 7.** The record leans on identifiers defined outside it: S01 to S07, C01 to C16, P01 to P16, H0 to H11, I-1 to I-4, K1 to K5, "the review, 4.3", "the definition, §18", "its D2", `BUILD-HANDOFF.md`. The reading note on the cover decodes the families, and the mechanism is followable, but sentences such as "its sign-in is ruled in (2026-10-23, delegated to the lead) as a quiet real moment" (A11), "steps 1 to 3 are true at birth there (its D2)" (11.1) and "proving isolation first on the surface with the most threats (C14, §18)" (12.3) carry meaning a reader without the packet cannot recover, and most sentences carry two to four such citations. A newcomer cannot "read the background section and follow any links to get the full context". Fix: in sections 1 to 5, keep the citation but say the thing (one clause each); drop the C and P ids where the record's own R and N ids already say it (section 4 cites both); demote H, I and K ids to the appendix references; cut the ones that cite another record's internal decision numbers ("its D2").

## 3. Three questions for the author

1. Which availability objective is the design's: 99.5% holding under one full restore at RTO (N2), or 99.5% assuming no full restore in a month (5.2)? And what are the two roles' 99.9% figures based on, given the fit check counts them as vendor-grade commitments?
2. Has the spike measured the DuckDB cold aggregate over a 1-million-record revision yet? N3's 2 s, the 1.8 s deadline, RK5 and D4's fallback all wait on it; who decides pre-aggregation at admission, and by when, so that the receipts' shape (one-way, D4) is settled before the demo's build?
3. Appendix D: were the invariant and the full threat statements dropped for length? Would you restore the invariant column across TH8 to TH28, and the full grammar on the rows the attack tree cites, so the 21 rows pass R6 on the same footing as the seven in 10.4?

## 4. Verdict

Not ready to deliver as it stands, but close, and nothing found is a design fault. The substance is strong: the problem is told as a mechanism with numbers, the quality targets are numbered and ranked to the optimisation sentence, every write path names its isolation level and its enforcer, the decisions carry reason and cost, the rollout and migration are concrete, the security section is the best part (invariants with enforcers, an entry-point table, fail-closed per control, operator access as design, SR ids with tests). Eighteen of the twenty-four R items pass outright. What sends it back is finishing, not thinking: the 21 appendix threat rows lack the invariant and the grammar the top seven have (R6); the three data-flow diagrams print their labels at about 4 pt and the ID columns of the STRIDE and asset tables and the cover break tokens mid-word; the body runs 48 pages against an L3 ceiling of 40; N2's objective is stated two incompatible ways; and the record cannot be read without the packet because nearly every sentence cites identifiers defined elsewhere. One length pass that also says things in place of citing them, a diagram re-render, the appendix D columns and the N2 sentence would make it deliverable at L3.
