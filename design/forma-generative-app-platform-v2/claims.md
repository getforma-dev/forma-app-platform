## Notes

- **VC3** Price List, US East (N. Virginia), PostgreSQL, db.m7g.large, OnDemand: "Multi-AZ 0.337 Hrs" (Single-AZ 0.168). The record's $0.378/hour is wrong; at $0.337 the instance is about $246/month, not $276, so the RDS line falls to roughly $290 and the 10x figure moves with it.
- **VC6** KMS pricing: "$0.15 per 10,000 requests" for ECC signing; asymmetric requests are excluded from the free tier; rotation adds a monthly fee "for first and second rotation of key material". The $0.03 rate covers only the symmetric data keys; the four per-role asymmetric keys bill their Sign/Verify at $0.15 per 10,000 and each yearly-rotated organization key costs up to $3/month after two rotations; the $13 line should say so or absorb it.
- **VC7** Pay-as-you-go: HTTP "$0.0075", HTTPS "$0.0100" per 10,000 requests (US, Mexico, Canada); always free: "10 million HTTP/HTTPS requests" a month. The record prices HTTPS at the HTTP rate; 10 million HTTPS requests are $10 at list and $0 inside the always-free allowance, so the $8 should become $10 (or $0 with the allowance named) and the 10x $75 about $90.
- **VC8** Data transfer out, us-east-1 to the internet: "0–10240 GB 0.09 per GB", then $0.085 from 10 TB to 50 TB; the first 100 GB a month are free; through CloudFront the first 1 TB is free, then "$0.085 per GB". For 100 GB leaving the ALB the list rate is $0.09/GB (inside the free 100 GB in practice); $0.085 is the 10–50 TB tier or CloudFront's rate, so the row should name which path the egress takes.
- **VC10** WAF pricing: "$5.00 per month" per web ACL, "$1.00 per rule per month" plus "$0.60 per million web requests". The record names only the request charge; one web ACL with a handful of rules adds $10 to $15 a month to the line.
- **VC11** VPC pricing page example: "$0.045 per hour" (given for US East (Ohio)); the us-east-1 Price List also lists "$0.045 per GB Data Processed by NAT Gateways". The hourly rate holds for N. Virginia, but the record omits the $0.045 per GB processing charge every egress byte to the provider and fAuth pays.
- **VC15** Pricing page: "Claude Opus 5.5 | $4 / MTok | ... | $20 / MTok"; Claude Opus 5, 4.8, 4.7, 4.6 and 4.5 are "$5 / MTok" in and "$25 / MTok" out. The figure holds only for Claude Opus 5.5; the record should name the model, since "Opus tier" also covers models priced 25% higher.
- **VC18** RDS: "Failover times are typically 60–120 seconds. However, large transactions or a lengthy recovery process can increase failover time." Replace "about a minute (assumed)" with the vendor's one to two minutes, and drill SLO1 against two minutes, not one (also 13.2, "RDS failover: a minute or longer").
- **VC23** KMS: when a key becomes unusable "the effect on data keys encrypted by the KMS key ... is delayed until the KMS key is used again". The vendor gives no time bound: an already-unwrapped data key stays usable for as long as the holder keeps it in memory, so AR6 should say "until the data key is next re-wrapped or the process restarts", not "for minutes" (the "within minutes" of VC24 is the key-state propagation, which is correct).
- **VC27** KMS: "Automatic key rotation is supported only on symmetric encryption KMS keys"; for asymmetric keys "you can rotate these KMS keys manually" by creating a new key. The yearly rotation of the per-role certifying key is a manual replacement (new key id, new policy and alias), not a KMS schedule; the record should say so or drop the year.
- **VC30** S3 documents "256-bit Advanced Encryption Standard (AES-256)" for server-side encryption and does not name the mode; AES-256-GCM is what KMS's SYMMETRIC_DEFAULT spec names for the KMS key itself. Write "AES-256 under SSE-KMS" (or cite GCM only for the envelopes the platform encrypts itself).
- **VC38** Route 53 documents a request interval of "every 10 seconds or every 30 seconds", a consecutive-failure threshold you choose, and the 18% healthy-checker rule; it states no end-to-end failover time, and the record's "three minutes" also depends on the record's TTL.
