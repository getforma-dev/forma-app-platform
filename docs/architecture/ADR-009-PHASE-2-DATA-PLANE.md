## Decisions

### D9.1 Parser packaging, concurrency and identity

The APP-004 parser ships as a **separate executable**, `parser-host`, built in the image from its own workspace and lockfile (`spikes/APP-004`, promoted to `parser/` by APP-017), as D7.10 kept DuckDB out of the Rust binary; this avoids Wasmtime 36's Rust 1.86 floor and the irreconcilable exact serde/serde_json/sha2 pins (map §5.3 #14). One `parser-host` process per job, spawned with `env_clear()` and only its pipes, so every job has its own `Engine`, epoch deadline and memory (map §5.5 #9). **Identity (m1):** the guest is built with `--remap-path-prefix` for `CARGO_HOME` and the checkout path; CI extracts `parser_guest.wasm` from the built image, runs the APP-004 hostile suite and `fixtures/qualify.py` against those exact bytes and records the digest in the release evidence; a release refuses an image whose parser digest has no passing record. The image's `parser-manifest.json` check at startup stays as an integrity check, not as the allowlist.

### D9.2 Import profile and capacity on the beta task (B5, B6)

The deployed profile is **demo A6**: CSV 10 MiB, XLSX 5 MiB compressed / 50 MiB expanded, 10,000 × 32 per sheet, 320,000 aggregate cells, every other cap the platform's (map §3.1). **B5 names the reduction of design A4:** the aggregate-cell cap applies to CSV too, so a CSV holds at most min(100,000, ⌊320,000 / columns⌋) records (64,000 for the five-column Northstar events); the platform's 1,000,000 records and a streaming output protocol are not built in PH02. **B6 names the reduction of design A5:** the task has **one job slot**, shared by `import` and `normalize` (M5), so one job runs per task and therefore per organization; the queue is 5 per organization and 20 per task, 429 with `Retry-After` beyond either, never silent loss. The queued state carries the queue position and an expected wait (position × the in-process p50 job duration), because the worst wait at the caps is long (m11).

**Memory budget on the 2 vCPU / 8 GiB task (M5).** With the result cache off (D9.7) the demo's 2 GiB cache line is freed:

| Component | Budget | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rust binary, including one decoded parser output (≤ 128 MiB JSON) | 1.5 GiB | measured by APP-017 at the output cap |
| Job slot: `parser-host` (2 GiB guest + host) **or** the normalize child, never both | 2.5 GiB | guest cap; RSS watch |
| Two query children (1 GiB `memory_limit` + interpreter each) | 2.5 GiB | `memory_limit`; RSS watch at 1.25 GiB each |
| Source cache, spill, scratch | 0 (task ephemeral disk) | 2 GiB temp per child |
| **Total** | **6.5 GiB** | 1.5 GiB headroom |

The binary samples each child's RSS every 100 ms and kills it at its line ("process memory watched by the host", design:303); a kill is a visible failure (`resource_limit`), never a partial value. A line is raised only from a recorded measurement and only within the 7 GiB total.

### D9.3 Object layout, normalization and determinism (B1, B8)

**B1 amends D7.10(2)'s object key** to the design's layout (map §5.3 #1), for every revision including APP-077's PH01 seeding: uploads stream to `o/{org}/staging/{upload_id}`; originals live at `o/{org}/s/{source}/r/{rev}/original`, normalized data at `o/{org}/s/{source}/r/{rev}/part.parquet`. An object already seeded at the D7.10 key is copied byte-identically to the B1 key by the operator (same digest). D7.10's cache, fetch and `normalized_digest` verification are otherwise unchanged.

**Lifecycle.** The `import` job parses the staged original and stores an inspection report at `o/{org}/staging/{upload_id}.inspection.json`; it creates no revision. Confirming a mapping (APP-018) enqueues a `normalize` job. **Normalization reads only parser output (M5, SR5):** it re-runs `parser-host` on the staged original with the confirmed sheet; when the parser has exited, the Rust binary applies the confirmed mapping and typing (the single source of truth) and streams typed rows to `python3 /app/runtime.py normalize` on stdin; DuckDB never opens the original. The normalize child runs under D7.10's confinement (`env_clear`, private scratch, `enable_external_access=false`, `lock_configuration`), `memory_limit='1GiB'`, `threads=1` and a 120 s deadline. It sorts by the confirmed time column then original coordinate and writes row groups of 100,000 with ZSTD under the pinned `duckdb==2.0.0`. The worker then PUTs `part.parquet`, copies the original from staging with `CopyObject` sending `x-amz-server-side-encryption: aws:kms` and the tenant key id explicitly (the bucket denies anything else, M7), and only after both return inserts the `source_revisions` row (ADR-002 object-before-row); staging objects are deleted after commit. A changed mapping is a new revision; nothing is rewritten. A staged original not confirmed within its 2-day expiry must be uploaded again.

**B8:** the worker writes its own job's Parquet object and inserts its `source_revisions` row; it reads no Parquet and no other revision, and the data role remains the only credential that reads Parquet.

**Digests (M6, option a).** `normalized_digest` stays SHA-256 of the Parquet object bytes, exactly what D7.10 verifies and PH01 rows hold. `source_revisions` gains `rows_digest genui.digest NULL` = `sha256("forma:normalized-rows:v1" ‖ 0x00 ‖ forma-json-v1 row stream in sorted order)` and `original_bytes bigint NULL`; both are NULL only for PH01 operator-seeded rows, and a RESTRICTIVE `WITH CHECK` for `genui_worker` requires both on every insert. `content_digest` is SHA-256 of the original bytes. **Column types:** text identifiers stay text; integers are `BIGINT` and a value outside int64 is unknown with its original text; money is `BIGINT` minor units plus a currency column (USD, exponent 2); timestamps are `TIMESTAMPTZ` UTC whole seconds. **Asset selection (map §5.2 #13):** `asset` is a confirmed text column of the events revision and is filtered as a typed `eq` parameter; PH01-pinned work keeps its operator-seeded revisions, PH02 imports create new revisions, and nothing rebinds (C05).

**Storage controls (M7, APP-016 owns them).** The tenant role gains `s3:DeleteObject` on `o/${tenant}/*` (staging cleanup and D9.12's delete; never `DeleteObjectVersion`). CDK adds lifecycle rules generated per tenant in the existing tenant loop: on `o/{t}/staging/`, current objects expire after 2 days, noncurrent versions after 1 day, incomplete multipart uploads after 1 day; on each trial organization's `o/{t}/`, current objects expire after 90 days (design A6) and noncurrent versions after 7 days; on the two fixture organizations' `o/{t}/s/`, noncurrent versions expire after 30 days (design:807). A failed job therefore leaves at most a staging object that **expires within 2 days**.

### D9.4 Jobs and worker authority (B2, B7, B9–B12)

**Scoped record.** `genui.jobs(organization_id, job_id, kind CHECK IN ('import','normalize'), state CHECK IN ('queued','running','failed','done','cancelled'), principal_id, membership_epoch_at_enqueue, idempotency_key, payload_digest, payload jsonb, claim_sequence bigint NULL, cancel_requested_at NULL, failure_code NULL, result_digest NULL, created_at, updated_at, PRIMARY KEY (organization_id, job_id), UNIQUE (organization_id, principal_id, idempotency_key))`, FORCE RLS with `tenant_scope`; it is the authoritative state the user sees. `membership_epoch_at_enqueue` is recorded for audit only (M2).

**Scheduling relations (B2).** `genui_private.job_queue(organization_id, job_id, kind, state CHECK IN ('queued','running'), claim_sequence genui.safe_counter NULL, lease_expires_at NULL, available_at, attempts smallint, cancel_requested boolean, PRIMARY KEY (organization_id, job_id))` with a partial index on queued rows and a **unique partial index on `state = 'running'`**, which makes "one job running per task" (B6) a database fact; and `genui_private.job_cursor(organization_id PRIMARY KEY, last_claimed_at)` for round-robin. Both are owned by `genui_owner`, have no RLS, hold no principal or payload, grant nothing to application roles, and are reachable only through the functions below. **B2 makes them the third and fourth exceptions** to ADR-004:13's RLS rule and ADR-007 A2.

**Definer `genui_jobs`.** NOLOGIN NOINHERIT NOSUPERUSER NOCREATEDB NOCREATEROLE NOREPLICATION NOBYPASSRLS, no IAM or application-role membership, created like `genui_admit` (`GRANT genui_jobs TO genui_migration WITH INHERIT FALSE, SET TRUE`, temporary `CREATE ON SCHEMA genui_private` revoked). It owns five `SECURITY DEFINER` functions with `SET search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp`, PUBLIC revoked; none sets scope or writes audit. `enqueue_job`, `release_job` and `request_job_cancel` run inside the caller's scoped transaction and require `current_setting('genui.organization_id') = p_org`; `claim_next_job` and `renew_job` run unscoped and touch only `job_queue` and `job_cursor`:

| Function | Behavior | EXECUTE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `enqueue_job(p_org, p_job, p_kind)` | requires the scoped `genui.jobs` row; locks the organization's `job_cursor` row (inserted on first use) `FOR UPDATE`, so the 5-per-organization cap is exact; refuses past 5 queued for the organization or 20 in all (the task cap may be exceeded by at most one concurrent enqueue, the workspace holding two connections); inserts the queue row; returns `queued` or `full` | `genui_workspace` |
| `claim_next_job(p_kinds)` | first looks for a `running` row: if one exists it is the only candidate (unexpired → returns nothing; expired → locked with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and reclaimed); only when no running row exists does it pick under `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` the queued, available row of the organization with the oldest `last_claimed_at` (re-review R-M1: picking another organization's queued row while an expired running row exists would collide on the unique running index and stall the queue); increments `claim_sequence` and `attempts`, sets `running` and a 120 s lease, stamps the cursor; returns `(organization_id, job_id, claim_sequence, attempts)`, or nothing while an unexpired running row exists; a concurrent claimer's unique violation (23505) on the running index or a lock wait past `lock_timeout` (55P03) is classified by storage as nothing claimed (D7.9; R-m6). The unique index means one running job per deployment, which equals one per task only under D7.18's single task | `genui_worker` |
| `renew_job(p_org, p_job, p_claim)` | extends the lease to now + 120 s if `claim_sequence` matches; returns `(renewed, cancel_requested)` | `genui_worker` |
| `release_job(p_org, p_job, p_claim)` | locks the queue row `FOR UPDATE`, compares `claim_sequence`, deletes the row; returns `(released, cancel_requested)` | `genui_worker` |
| `request_job_cancel(p_org, p_job)` | locks the queue row `FOR UPDATE`; if queued, deletes it and sets `genui.jobs.state = 'cancelled'`; if running, sets `cancel_requested`; stamps `genui.jobs.cancel_requested_at`; returns `cancelled`, `requested` or `absent` | `genui_workspace` |

Grants of `genui_jobs`: USAGE on schemas `genui` and `genui_private`; SELECT(`kind`) as well as the columns below on `genui.jobs`; SELECT, INSERT, DELETE and UPDATE(`state`, `claim_sequence`, `lease_expires_at`, `available_at`, `attempts`, `cancel_requested`) on `job_queue`; SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE(`last_claimed_at`) on `job_cursor`; SELECT(`organization_id`, `job_id`, `state`) and UPDATE(`state`, `cancel_requested_at`, `updated_at`) on `genui.jobs` (reached under the caller's scope). **B11** extends ADR-007 A3's normalization to `genui_jobs` (eight fixed roles) in the same migration, with `catalog-query.json` and `qualify_tenant_schema.py` updated and re-pinned.

**Transactions (B7).** Every step is its own transaction inside ADR-004's 3 s; none spans the job's work.
1. **Enqueue** (workspace, after the D9.9 transfer): precheck `import_source` (D9.5), member fence, entitlement and D9.13 limits; insert `sources` (upload only) and `genui.jobs`; `enqueue_job`; audit `job.enqueued`; commit. `full` rolls back and answers 429. An identical `Idempotency-Key` returns the existing job; a different payload under it is 409.
2. **Claim** (worker, no scope): `claim_next_job`; commit.
3. **Start** (worker, `WorkerScope`): copy `claim_sequence` and `running` into `genui.jobs`; recheck authority; commit. A row on its fourth claim is instead failed with `worker_lost` and released (the dead-letter rule, m8).
4. **Heartbeat** every 30 s: `renew_job`; a refused renewal stops the work; `cancel_requested` stops it before its irreversible step.
5. **Result** (worker, `WorkerScope`): recheck authority; `release_job` (stale `claim_sequence` → rollback, the late result refused); compare `genui.jobs.claim_sequence`; write the revision (normalize only), state `done` or `failed` with `failure_code`, and one audit event; commit. If `release_job` reports a cancel request, the transaction writes `cancelled` instead and no revision: cancellation before the result commits stops the work; after it, the job reports `done` (C07).

A worker that dies loses its lease at 120 s and the job is reclaimed; its late result fails step 5. Imports never take `organization_sequences`, so D7.7's lock order is unchanged.

**Worker authority (B9, M2).** `WorkerScope` is a storage-private type built only from a claimed `(organization, job)`: it installs the organization, reads the job's principal and that member's *current* epoch under scope, installs both, and takes `lock_member_authority` (**B9** grants EXECUTE on it to `genui_worker`, issued as `genui_lock`, its owner since 0002). The epoch is read and the fence taken in one statement, so a sign-out between them cannot stop the job spuriously (R-m3). If the recheck fails at start or at result, the job ends `failed` with `failure_code = authority_revoked`, no revision is written, the queue row is released, and the `job.failed` event is written on the `worker` chain (R-m3). It is never a `TrustedRequest`. At start and at result the shared `precheck` accepts `Role::Worker` only through `WorkerScope` and only for `import_source` on the organization, checking organization active, membership active, entitlement active and unexpired, and the permission. The epoch is not compared with the enqueue value, so sign-out and unrelated grants do not cancel jobs; an expired or revoked trial does (APP-015:42).

**Grants (B10).** `genui_worker`: USAGE on `genui_private`; SELECT on `memberships`, `organization_permissions`, `source_permissions`, `entitlements`, `sources`, `source_revisions`, `genui.jobs` and its own `audit_heads` row; UPDATE(`state`, `claim_sequence`, `failure_code`, `result_digest`, `updated_at`) on `genui.jobs`; INSERT on `source_revisions` and `audit_events`; UPDATE(`chain_sequence`, `head_hash`) on its own `audit_heads` row; EXECUTE on `lock_member_authority`, `claim_next_job`, `renew_job`, `release_job`. `genui_workspace` gains SELECT and INSERT on `genui.jobs`, INSERT on `sources`, and EXECUTE on `enqueue_job` and `request_job_cancel`.

**Audit (B12).** A sixth chain `worker` (`genui_worker`) with kinds `job.completed`, `job.failed`, `job.cancelled`; the `writer_role` CHECK is broadened, provisioning creates six heads, and APP-015's migration creates the `worker` genesis head for every existing organization under that organization's scope. The workspace chain gains `job.enqueued`, `job.cancel_requested` and `job.cancelled`. An event is written on the chain of the role whose transaction made the transition: a queued job cancelled by the user is `job.cancelled` on `workspace`; a running job stopped on request is `job.cancel_requested` on `workspace`, then `job.cancelled` on `worker`. `audit_events` gains nullable `job_id` and `source_id`; canonical event bytes include a new column only when it is non-NULL, so every existing hash verifies unchanged.

**Visibility (map §5.2 #9).** The upload response carries the committed `queued` state, position and expected wait, which `enqueue_job` returns alongside `queued` (its organization position and the task-wide count ahead, computed under its cursor lock; R-m4); the source card polls `GET /v1/jobs/{job}` every 500 ms (readable by the job's principal, or by a member holding `import_source` on the organization, after the shared precheck and fence; others get the uniform 404; R-m5) while the job is not terminal and the card is visible. "Visible within 1 s" means the browser shows a committed transition within 1 s at p50 under healthy connectivity; APP-019 measures it. **Operations (m8):** terminal `genui.jobs` rows are kept 90 days (A6) and purged by APP-064; the read-only operator subcommand `jobs --stuck` (B15) lists running rows with expired leases and queued rows older than 10 minutes (AL6) and ships with APP-015.

### D9.5 Creator authority, import authority and sharing (B13, B14)

**B13.** `organization_permissions.permission` broadens to `admin_policy` or `import_source`; `import_source` is granted through `code_grant_templates` (it is not refused there) or the operator, and is required at upload, enqueue, start and result. `sources` gains `created_by uuid NULL`, `creator_revoked_at NULL` and `display_name`; `saved_work` gains `created_by uuid NULL` and `creator_revoked_at NULL`; each `created_by` has a composite foreign key `(organization_id, created_by) → memberships`, is NULL only for rows that predate the migration, and has a RESTRICTIVE `WITH CHECK (created_by = nullif(current_setting('genui.principal_id', true), '')::uuid)` for the inserting role. Creator authority, with `creator_revoked_at IS NULL`, equals `source_read` on that source, and `record_read` and `record_write` (not `share_work` or `export`) on that work; it replaces only the `work_permissions` lookup, so the app `run` clause and the per-dependency `source_read`-or-creator clause still apply. No permission row is written and no epoch advances at creation, so no open display is invalidated (map §5.1 #8). **Revocation:** the operator subcommand `revoke-creator-authority` (B15) sets `creator_revoked_at` under ADR-004:34's order (organization `FOR SHARE`, member `FOR UPDATE`, epoch + 1) with `creator_authority.revoked` on the `operator` chain; membership revocation ends it through `precheck`. Any future reinstatement path must set `creator_revoked_at` on that member's rows in its own transaction.

**B14.** Member-initiated sharing (R11, design §7 step 6) is deferred: in PH02 a permission row for another member is written only by the operator subcommands `grant-permission` and `revoke-permission` (B15; closed: `record_read`, `record_write`, `source_read`, `import_source`), each under ADR-004:34's fence and epoch advance with `permission.granted` / `permission.revoked` on the `operator` chain. Member sharing arrives with APP-048 (PH04), before act 5 is recorded.

### D9.6 Contract edition `impl-…2`

APP-015 opens the edition (`evidence/APP-015/contract-edition/`), retaining every prior vector and reproducing the new ones in Rust and JavaScript: job state `cancelled`, `cancel_requested`, `queue_position` and `expected_wait_seconds`; `coverage` (`complete`, `partial`) on input contracts and source revisions; `rows_digest` on source revisions; per-operator `{id, version}` and per-renderer `{kind, version}` lists in the canonical publication envelope and in receipts (m5: the scalar `publications.operator_version`/`renderer_version` columns stay as the registry edition and their CHECK broadens to `≥ 1`; no child relation); `exact_rational` `{numerator, denominator}` of ≤ 38-digit integers in lowest terms; a window-derived denominator `{window_minutes}`; `partial` meaning partial coverage only; `stale` meaning a display older than its authorization freshness (ADR-002); `mapping_version` in receipt source entries; problem codes `source_deleted`, `source_quarantined`, `unknown_value_in_aggregate`, `lookup_ambiguous`, `resource_limit`, and 429 bodies naming the D9.13 limit; parser refusal codes as job `failure_code`s. Currency stays USD at exponent 2.

### D9.7 Query execution (B3)

Rust is the single source of truth for the grammar: admission and typing happen in `app/src/admission` and the query module, which compiles an admitted query into a closed JSON **plan** (operators and typed parameters, never SQL text); the Python executor maps the plan onto DuckDB's relational API with bound parameters; shared vectors check both sides. **Filters travel only in POST bodies** (`POST /v1/work/{app}/{work}/queries/{view}` carries filter and epoch); APP-003's `GET /linked?filter=` is not ported and a query string on a `/v1` query route is refused 400 (design:786).

**Operators the first tickets need (map §5.1 #5).** Each source input is its revision's Parquet file in the organization's cache; up to 8 inputs per plan; record inputs per D9.8. `cidr_exact_then_longest` runs on integers: IPv4 addresses and networks are normalized to unsigned 32-bit values and prefix lengths, the match is `(ip & mask) = network`, exact beats the longest prefix, an equal-length duplicate refuses with `lookup_ambiguous`, no inet extension. A ratio never uses DuckDB `/`: the engine returns exact integer numerator and denominator (sums as `HUGEINT`, sent as decimal strings, > 38 digits refused) and Rust reduces them to an `exact_rational`; `window_minutes` comes from the admitted half-open window, which must be a whole number of minutes. A sum, count or ratio over a column holding any unknown value is refused with `unknown_value_in_aggregate`, never computed over the rest (m9).

**Admission (B3, amends D7.10(3) and design §6.3).** The task runs **2** queries at once; an organization may hold both while no other organization waits (work-conserving), and dequeue is round-robin across organizations, so a waiting organization takes the next free slot (APP-024:38). A new filter epoch cancels that work's queued older-epoch queries before it is admitted; running ones finish within 1.8 s and are discarded. Queue 12 per organization, 20 per task; 429 with `Retry-After` beyond. A six-view refresh is therefore three waves. **N3:** a per-request child costs about 230 ms (map §3.13), so three waves are about 0.7 s, over the 500 ms p50; PH02 therefore requires D7.10(5)'s supervised broker (two active), adopted by APP-024 if APP-012 has not adopted it. N3 stays a target measured, not promised (demo N3).

**Caches (M11).** The **result cache is off** in PH02 (C06 makes it optional; demo:350). The source cache is keyed by `(organization_id, normalized_digest)` inside D7.10's per-organization directory, released to an engine only after reauthorization, and evicted on deletion or tombstone. APP-024's cache sentences are proved on the source cache and on stored receipt lookup by digest: reauthorization before a cached source reaches an engine; a foreign or revoked receipt lookup returns the uniform 404; no engine slot is reserved for inactive or pre-access sessions. Its result-cache wording is deferred with this reason (D9.14 records the ticket amendment).

**Confinement (M4).** Each engine child keeps D7.10's controls plus `RLIMIT_NOFILE` 64 and `RLIMIT_CORE` 0, both per process; APP-012's regression runs S02's six views and the 1,000,000-row aggregate under them, and a limit that fails is raised by measurement, never removed silently. **`RLIMIT_NPROC` is not used:** it counts every thread of UID 65532, so the app's own threads would starve the child. **`RLIMIT_AS` is not used:** DuckDB's and the interpreter's address-space reservations are unmeasured; D9.2's RSS watch is the memory bound. Both, with the absence of a network namespace inside Fargate (map §5.1 #15), are recorded limitations.

### D9.8 Receipts and record snapshots (X1; B4 withdrawn)

**Records into a query (map §5.1 #6).** D7.6 is unchanged: the data role reads in `READ COMMITTED READ WRITE`, takes the member and work fences first (ADR-004:22, :26), then reads the snapshot sequence and the record rows in **one statement**: a CTE selects `next_commit_sequence − 1` from `organization_sequences` as S and the latest revision of each record with `commit_sequence ≤ S`. One READ COMMITTED statement sees one snapshot, and commands commit in sequence order (D7.7's row lock), so S and the rows agree; the transaction commits and execution runs outside it. Any further statement (drilldown pages, re-derivation) filters by `commit_sequence ≤ S` from the receipt; committed revisions are immutable, so those reads are stable. The binary passes the rows to the engine child on stdin as typed forma-json-v1 lines with the plan, loaded into a temporary table; a record input is bounded at 100,000 rows and 64 MiB (design:313), and anything larger, or a read past `statement_timeout`, is refused (`narrow_the_query`, `resource_limit`), never partial.

**Receipts (M10).** `genui.query_receipts(organization_id, receipt_id, digest UNIQUE per organization, canonical_receipt, cited_by_outcome NULL, created_at)` is created by APP-013's migration 0007, as ADR-007 assigns, with this column set; APP-023 adds only columns (`mapping_version` source entries live in the canonical receipt). Receipts are stored only when cited by a committed record command or an explicit "keep", at most one per commit. Drilldown is a reproducible predicate with 500-row pages. **Quarantine (m4):** `source_revisions.quarantined_at NULL`; the operator subcommand `verify-receipts` (B15, APP-023) re-derives cited receipts and on a mismatch sets it with `revision.quarantined` on the `operator` chain; a query against a quarantined revision is refused with `source_quarantined`.

### D9.9 Upload transport (B17)

`POST /v1/sources/uploads`: `Content-Length` required and checked against D9.2's cap before any byte is read, else **413 `payload_too_large`** (**B17**: this 413 replaces the demo's 422 `narrow_the_query` for the oversize XLSX wherever the size is declared; a cap met while parsing — expanded bytes, cells, rows, time, memory, formula, macro — ends the job `failed` with the parser's refusal code on the source card and no revision); `Content-Type` `text/csv` or the XLSX media type; same-origin check and the session's synchronizer token in a header; required `Idempotency-Key`. **Order (M8):** a short authorization transaction (precheck `import_source`, fence, entitlement, D9.13 limits) commits; the body streams to `o/{org}/staging/{upload_id}` with SSE-KMS under the tenant key while SHA-256 and length are computed; after the PUT returns and both match, D9.4's enqueue transaction runs with `upload_id`, `content_digest`, format and byte length in the payload. No database transaction is open during the transfer and nothing is buffered whole. The route's deadline is its own: 120 s absolute with a floor of 64 KiB/s over any 10 s window; D7.14's body limits and 5 s handler deadline do not apply to it. The browser shows progress from its own request.

### D9.10 Presentation schema

`genui.personal_view_state(organization_id, principal_id, work_id, layout jsonb ≤ 64 KiB, filters jsonb ≤ 16 KiB, updated_at)`, with a RESTRICTIVE policy `USING` and `WITH CHECK (principal_id = current principal)` (m2), written by the workspace role and not audited; `genui.shared_views(organization_id, view_id, work_id, audience CHECK ('work_readers'), layout, filters, created_by, created_at)` with `view.shared`; `genui.aliases(organization_id, alias_normalized, target_kind, target_id, created_at, retired_at, UNIQUE (organization_id, alias_normalized) WHERE retired_at IS NULL)`, created and retired only with `admin_policy` (R16), with `alias.created` and `alias.retired`; `apps.display_name`. All three kinds are on the `workspace` chain (B12). All relations are workspace-owned, FORCE RLS, grants in the owning ticket's migration (B10).

### D9.11 Canvas budget (B16)

**B16 amends ADR-001:18 and :20** for the canvas route only: its target moves from 28,100 B to the framework's enforced 28,420 B cap, and renderers beyond the canvas core may load as one native group under ADR-001's 40,708 B Forma / 64 KiB ceiling. ### D9.12 Deleted sources

Saved work whose dependency revision is deleted still opens: the work precheck no longer denies the whole work (`authorization.rs:174`), each affected view renders a tombstone with the historical identity and digests, and a query against the deleted revision is refused with `source_deleted`; nothing rebinds (S06, C05). **Application delete:** the source's creator or a holder of `admin_policy` deletes a source; one transaction sets `deleted_at` on its revisions (workspace UPDATE(`deleted_at`) on `source_revisions`, B10) with `source.deleted` on the `workspace` chain; after commit the binary evicts the `(organization, digest)` cache entries (m3) and deletes the objects, whose noncurrent versions expire per D9.3. A revision whose object is missing is treated as deleted.

### D9.13 Beta usage limits per entitlement

Defaults: **20 imports per day** (`import` jobs created in the last 24 hours, except those cancelled while queued), **200 MiB of stored originals** (sum of `original_bytes` over non-deleted revisions created by the member, so D9.12's delete frees quota), **6,000 queries per hour** (demo A4's 4,320 per member with margin), and the D9.2/D9.7 queues. Exceeding a limit answers 429 naming it. Overrides live in `genui.entitlement_limits(organization_id, entitlement_id, imports_per_day, stored_original_bytes, queries_per_hour)`, FORCE RLS, readable by workspace and data (B10), written only by the operator subcommand `set-limits` (B15) with `limits.set` on the `operator` chain; an absent row means the defaults, so `admit_redemption` is unchanged. Daily and storage counts are durable (`genui.jobs`, `source_revisions`); the hourly count is in-process (D7.5).

## Victor's veto

**B18 — arbitrary uploads on a synthetic beta (M14).** ADR-008 §1 lets invitees use the beta "on synthetic data", design A12 says synthetic only, yet D9.9 accepts any CSV or XLSX. GenUI's default, which stands unless Victor vetoes it:
- **Beta copy** on the invitation landing and above the Upload control: upload synthetic or non-sensitive data only; uploads are deleted when the trial ends.
- **Per-upload attestation:** the upload request must carry the attestation header; it is recorded in the job payload, so it is digest-covered and audited.
- **Retention purge:** the operator subcommand `purge-organization-objects` (B15) runs within 7 days after a trial organization's last entitlement ends, or on request, tombstoning its revisions with `organization.objects_purged` on the `operator` chain and deleting its objects, whose noncurrent versions expire 7 days later (D9.3); D9.3's 90-day lifecycle on trial prefixes is the backstop.
- **Application delete:** D9.12's member-visible source delete.

The alternative is to restrict beta imports to the fixture files until a data-class policy exists (A12's "If wrong").
