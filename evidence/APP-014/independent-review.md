## What I examined

**The race orders (D7.9).** Every race is driven by one armed checkpoint (`TestHooks::arm`, one-shot: `pool.rs:121-137` takes the phase on reaching it, notifies `reached`, awaits `release`) plus a poll of `pg_stat_activity` (`lock_wait`). The command path's order is claim → member fence → work fence → sequence `FOR UPDATE` → record `FOR UPDATE` (`commands.rs:954-1075`), as D7.9 says with the claim "immediately after the shared precheck and before the fences". The harness exercises: same key behind an in-flight claim (`race_qualification.rs:539-560`, replay), same key with a changed payload (`:563-589`, `payload_mismatch`), two different keys on one record (`:647-725`, `expected_version`, recovery refused by the M1 rule), both over the route past `lock_timeout` (`:731-807`, 409 `in_flight` then 200 / 409 `conflict` / recovery commits); adoption first at `precheck` (`:1017-1030`, `Stale` v2), adoption held while the claim's `saved_work` reference waits (`:1041-1052`, 412 naming v3), adoption held past timeout as `FOR UPDATE` → `in_flight` and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` → `busy` (`:1065-1078`), command first holding the work `FOR SHARE` (`:1089-1131`, commit on v3, adoption to v4, replay keeps `work_version` 3, new edit 412 → recovery on 4), claim-first (`:1145-1160`); the same five shapes for revocation (`:1178-1448`) with the real `revoke-membership` for the unheld orders and fixture-only SQL in the D7.1 order for the held ones, plus the delivery-after-fence order (`:1417-1448`). Both "earlier authorized commit remains recorded" checks assert the revision/outcome/event on the database and read the record back as another member (`:1332-1345`), and "current visibility governs delivery" is asserted as `Denied` at the old and the new epoch and 404 on the route (`:1347-1366`). The lock order is also what makes the claim-first interleavings deadlock-free: the writer queues `FOR UPDATE` behind the claim's foreign-key `KEY SHARE`, the command upgrades to `FOR SHARE` and both complete (asserted by completion of both tasks within their timeouts; a deadlock would have surfaced as 40P01 → one of them failing).

**The `pg_stat_activity` observations.** `lock_wait(fixture, n)` (`admission_qualification.rs:169-182`) polls `SELECT count(*) FROM pg_stat_activity WHERE datname='genui' AND wait_event_type='Lock'` every 10 ms until the count is `>= n`, with a 10 s timeout. It therefore proves that a backend of the `genui` database entered a heavyweight-lock wait while the other actor held its locks — a genuine concurrency observation, and the right barrier (no sleep). It does not identify the waiting backend, the blocking backend, the lock type or the relation. See m1.

**Revocation held across a checkpoint** (`:1216-1237`, `:1251-1263`, `:1269-1282`). The held writer is `hold()` (`:239-249`): `BEGIN; SET LOCAL ROLE genui_owner; set_config(organization)`, `organizations FOR SHARE`, member `FOR UPDATE`, `UPDATE status='revoked', membership_epoch+1`, left open; the paused route command has already passed `precheck` at the old epoch, its claim insert then waits on the member row's `KEY SHARE`, the writer commits, the fence (`lock_member_authority`) sees the revoked row and refuses: 404, tally `[0,0,0]`, claims unchanged. This is the ADR-004 "precheck then fence" guarantee under the exact adverse interleaving. The held-past-timeout pair proves D7.9's two rows (`in_flight` for `FOR UPDATE` at the claim's reference, `busy` for `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` at the fence) and `never 503` (`problem()` asserts `!= 503` on every refusal, `:213-230`). The restore between orders is fixture-only SQL in the D7.1 order with an epoch bump (`:284-288`), and the harness re-reads the epoch and re-mints the session each time, so no stale epoch leaks into a later check.

**Restart/reopen of the same record/version/outcome** (`:500-530`). `stores.close()`, `drop`, new `Stores`, new `Auth` (new in-process vault), new router; the old cookie answers 404 — by `resolve_session`'s "vault no longer holds the refresh reference → delete the row, `Ended`" branch (`sessions.rs:784-789`); a fresh session's retry of the lost key answers byte-identical to the original 200, the lookup too, storage `Replayed(o1)`, tally still `[2,2,2]`. The database is not restarted (declared in the README and the report's limits).

**The M1-rule rework** (`e3b4119`). Each race that needs an admissible edit now opens its own record (`record(n)`, `:53-55`); the C/D race is on record 6 opened at v1 so both can fire the transition, and the recovery on v2 is asserted **refused** (`:710-718`), which is the M1 rule's consequence, not a weakening of the race. The new check (`:591-641`) races a note-only edit and the undeclared reverse transition on the exhausted record: both `Denied`, three route posts 404, tally `[2,2,2]`, claims and `next_commit_sequence` unchanged — proving that a refusal releases the claim and does not consume a sequence number. The group's seven commits are tallied per record (`:803-806`: 2, 2, 1, 2). The closing check derives 17 from the database (`:1455-1466`) and I counted 17 commits in the source (2 + 7 + 3 + 3 + 2).

**Check text versus assertions.** I compared every check string with the assertions that precede it. Apart from the lock-identity clauses (m1) and the parenthetical "the old session ended with its vault" (asserted through the 404 only; the row deletion is production code, not a database assertion here), each clause has an assertion behind it. ## Findings (ranked)

**m1 (minor) — the check texts name the awaited lock as "observed", but the harness observes only a `Lock` wait somewhere in the database.** Thirteen call sites (`race_qualification.rs:551, 577, 618, 685, 1046, 1071, 1093, 1149, 1232, 1260, 1273, 1319, 1393`) use `lock_wait(&fixture, 1)`, and ten check strings (`:560, 589, 641, 725, 1052, 1131, 1160, 1237, 1368, 1412`) assert in prose that the second actor "waits on the first's in-flight claim", "on the organization sequence row", "on the member row", "on the adoption writer's saved-work lock" or "on the claim's key share" **(observed)**. Which lock was waited on follows from the D7.9 order and from the result code (`in_flight` vs `busy` do discriminate claim/sequence from fence), not from the observation; and `lock_wait` has no precondition of zero waiters (only `:850` asserts `lock_waiters == 0`), so it would also be satisfied by an unrelated waiter (none exists in the current sequence of checks; every earlier waiter is awaited to completion). The ticket's items do not depend on the identity of the lock — the write counts and typed results carry them — so this is wording plus a cheap strengthening, not a hole in the proof. **Fix:** replace `lock_wait(&fixture, 1)` with a helper that returns the observation and assert it; I verified the query on PostgreSQL 16.13 (same image digest): `SELECT a.pid, a.wait_event, l.relation::regclass::text, pg_blocking_pids(a.pid) FROM pg_stat_activity a LEFT JOIN pg_locks l ON l.pid=a.pid AND l.locktype='tuple' WHERE a.datname='genui' AND a.wait_event_type='Lock'`. For a row-lock wait (the claim's foreign-key `KEY SHARE` against a held `FOR UPDATE`, the sequence row, the record row, the writers' `FOR UPDATE` behind a `FOR SHARE`/`KEY SHARE`) the waiter holds a granted `tuple` lock whose `relation` names the table (`genui.memberships`, `genui.saved_work`, `genui.organization_sequences`), so assert the relation; for the same-key wait on an in-flight claim (a unique-index wait) there is no tuple lock — `wait_event` is `transactionid` and `pg_blocking_pids` names exactly the paused command's backend, so assert `wait_event='transactionid'` and one blocker that is `idle in transaction`. Assert `0` waiters before spawning the second actor in every race, and put the observation (pid, wait event, relation, blockers) into each check's report entry. Then the texts are earned as written.

**m2 (minor) — the report records pass flags only.** Each entry of `race-campaign.json` is `{"name", "passed"}`; the only observed value kept is `session_wait_observation`. "Run 1 and run 2 … identical results" (README `:17`, `:49`) therefore means identical check names and flags, which I confirmed for the two evidence runs and my two runs, but a reader cannot see the statuses, versions, epochs, tallies or waiter counts the assertions compared. **Fix:** have `Group::check` take the observed values (the tallies, `current_version`, `work_version`, the reply status/code, the epoch pair, the lock observation from m1) and write them beside `name`/`passed`, as the APP-013 report does with `first_outcome`.

**n1 (nit) — `evidence/APP-014/command-campaign.json` is the 20-check run at `ea8ba23`**, while the README's text describes the 21-check run at the tip as "kept in scratch". The 21-check report exists in the repository at `evidence/APP-013/review-fixes/command-campaign.json`; link it, or copy it here.

Not findings, recorded for the owner: the README's `cargo test` counts (117 / 13, bin 5) are stale at `14dc68d` (130 / 13, bin 5) because later slices merged; `fmt --check` is clean at this tip; the fixture-only held writers and restorations (`genui_owner` SQL in the D7.1 order) are disclosed in the README and the report's limits and are the only way to hold a lock across a checkpoint without changing the operator package.

## Claims I failed to refute

One intended write per race under same-key and different-key contention, on the storage path and over the route (tallies per record, total claims, 17 = 17 = 17 at the close); the replay of a lost response byte-identical after a stale premise, a revoke/grant cycle and a full pool/store restart; the recovery view's contents (`payload_mismatch` with the stored outcome; `expected_version` with the winner's values and `stored_outcome: null`); a refused or raced command leaving no claim, outcome, event or sequence increment; `Retry-After: 2` on both waiting rows and never a 503 on any refusal; the claim preceding the fences (a held `FOR UPDATE` is met at the claim's reference → `in_flight`; a `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` is met at the fence → `busy`), in both the adoption and the revocation shape; both claim-first interleavings completing without deadlock; the earlier authorized commit surviving a later revocation or adoption and being readable by another member while the revoked principal is refused at both epochs; delivery of a stored outcome refused when the revocation commits between precheck and fence; another principal's identical key being its own scope at the row level (RLS counts) and at the result level; the operator's `verify` agreeing with the database counts after 17 commits; two runs of mine and two of the implementer's producing the same 26 results.
