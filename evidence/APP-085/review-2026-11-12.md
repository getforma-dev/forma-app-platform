## Questions asked

**Is the publish rule correct and free of races?** Yes.
- The newest-`policy_kind` read (`definitions.rs:1168`) runs on `permit.transaction()`. That is the transaction opened by `Transaction::begin_serializable` (`authorization.rs:196`, `BEGIN ISOLATION LEVEL SERIALIZABLE READ WRITE`). The read comes after the fence. It precedes the control-head `FOR UPDATE` (`definitions.rs:1183`) and the INSERT (`:1218`).
- Consider a concurrent `independent_review` publication that commits after this attempt's snapshot. It has advanced the same `audit_heads` control row, so this attempt's `FOR UPDATE` raises 40001. The single retry then reads with a fresh snapshot, sees the review and returns `Denied`. SSI also covers the predicate read on `publications`, so the outcome does not depend on the head lock alone.
- The opposite order serializes too: owner first, reviewer second. The final state is "under review".
- A stale snapshot cannot produce a false `Denied`. Under the rule, nothing can make an app's newest publication `owner_publish` again once it is `independent_review`.
- No campaign races an owner against a reviewer. The serialization of the head lock has existing proof in the review M1 cases of the same campaign. I accept the race-freedom by inspection on that basis.

**Are the other publish entry points covered?** Yes.
- Every caller goes through `Control::publish` → `publish_until` → `publish_once`: `composer/routes.rs:636`, `composer/presentation.rs:271` and `boxes/data_copy.rs:286`, plus the test `work_presentation_checks.rs:26`.
- `definitions.rs:1218` is the only Rust `INSERT INTO genui.publications`.
- Only `genui_control` holds INSERT on the table (`0005:59-61`). The other inserts are the operator seed (`tenant_seed.py:179`) and test fixtures.

**Is the composer change correct?** Yes.
- The added subquery uses the same `ORDER BY publication_sequence DESC LIMIT 1` as the digest subquery, in the same statement and snapshot. It is served by `UNIQUE (organization_id, app_id, publication_sequence)`.
- In `prepare_composer` the read runs after the per-member advisory lock (`composer.rs:285-293`).
- The composer transaction is READ COMMITTED. An app can move into review between the choice and the publish. If that happens, the publish rule refuses it (`Denied` → `Refused::NotFound`, `routes.rs:1019`). The composer cannot bypass the rule; it fails closed.

**Can the skip create shells repeatedly?** No.
- A shell is inserted only when no owned app is eligible. The helper reuses any unpublished owned app that has `edit_definition` and `run` (`0016:46-52`). Concurrent first composes serialize on the advisory lock. APP-145's queued-compose ordering case still passes.
- Once owner-published, the shell is eligible and is reused. The campaign proves this at `composer_qualification.rs:619-638` and `:887-891`.
- Another shell would follow only if a reviewer moved the shell itself into review. That needs an operator to grant `review_publish` on that specific shell, so a member cannot drive it.

**Does the skip change read-only listings?** No.
- Only `authoring` changes. `library` and `sources` are untouched.
- The read-only consumers use `library` and `sources` only: `commands.rs:237`, `mcp/tools.rs:464,745`, `actions/resolve.rs:249`, `actions/routes.rs`, `slack/flows.rs`.
- One behaviour change on the compose path is not in the log. The matcher excludes the authoring app (`routes.rs:322`). Before, Priya's reviewed Northstar app was excluded. Now it is a matcher candidate for retrieval, and her shell is excluded instead. Retrieval writes no publication, so this has no security effect.

**Are the campaign edits honest?** Yes. Nothing weakens a security assertion.
- The no-key compose now asserts its exact footprint: the shell only, +1 audit, +1 app, +3 grants (`:274-288`). The old campaign took `before` *after* that compose, so it never asserted the "writes nothing" claim. This is a strengthening.
- Generated work is asserted to land on the shell, and the reviewed `APP` gains no publication (`:363-393`).
- Adapted work is asserted on the shell (`:500-503`).
- The counts of apps owned rise from 1 to 2, which is correct.
- The nonexistent-policy case in the definition campaign was already `Denied` before APP-085. It is a regression guard, and the ticket claims fail-before only for the `owner_publish` case. Both claimed fail-before cases are reproduced above.

**Is the ADR amendment accurate?** Yes, and it appends.
- The diff adds 22 lines after the last table and removes none.
- Each statement matches the code. The one exception is "keeping land[s] on the shell" (`ADR-007:170`): the campaign implies it but does not assert it (m6).

**Is "newest" the right rule?** In my judgement, "any `independent_review` publication in the app's history" is the better rule. See m1.

## Findings

No Blocker. No Major.

**m1. "Newest" keeps apps already bypassed out of review.** `definitions.rs:1168`, `composer.rs:298`.
- Under the new rule, review only ever tightens, so for every history this code can produce, "newest" and "ever" agree.
- They differ only for histories the bypass itself wrote before this lands. The ticket log says the pre-fix composer did exactly that: it owner-published Priya's composes over the reviewed Northstar app. Presentation edits and data copies could do the same.
- In any persistent database where that happened (a long-lived local profile, or beta if it was used that way), Northstar's newest publication is now `owner_publish`. The publish rule therefore lets the owner publish without review, and `choose_authoring` selects the app again. The log's behaviour change ("Priya's composes go to her own shell") then holds only on fresh databases.
- **Fix:** replace the `ORDER BY … LIMIT 1` reads with `EXISTS (… AND policy_kind='independent_review')`, in both places. The index is the same. No fresh-seed or campaign result changes, because the seeded reviewed apps have one publication.
- **Alternative:** record in the ADR that such histories are grandfathered, and have someone with access check live data with a read-only query. I made no such query.

**m2. Presentation and data-copy editing do not know the rule.** `work_presentation.rs:72`, `boxes/data_copy.rs:243`.
- `can_edit` checks the policy of the *work's pinned* publication, not the app's newest. Take an app that a reviewer moved into review after it was owner-published. Works still pinned to the earlier `owner_publish` publication show `can_edit = true`.
- The edit then writes a definition revision and a `definition.drafted` event. The publish is `Denied`, and the route answers 404 (`presentation.rs:286`).
- This is secure: the bypass that existed here before APP-085 is closed. The cost is an offered action that always fails, plus an orphan draft on every attempt.
- **Fix:** add the app's newest (or, per m1, any) publication kind to `can_edit`.

**m3. A reviewer's move into review is one-way, and the ADR does not say who can make it.** `ADR-007:160`.
- "Moving an app to a stricter policy stays allowed" means any member that the operator granted `review_publish` on an app can move an owner's app into review with one publish. This removes the owner's self-publication and the composer's authoring on that app.
- Nothing reverses it: no member action and no operator command.
- **Fix:** record both facts in the amendment. Name who would own a governed "leave review" path (`admin_policy` or box 2's operator work), or state that none exists.

**m4. The rule ignores later policy versions; `can_edit` does not.**
- The publish rule keys on the *stamped* kind of the newest publication. `work_presentation.rs:72` also consults the *current* kind of that publication's policy id (`get(7)`).
- `work_presentation_checks.rs:162-163` already inserts a newer `independent_review` version of an `owner_publish` policy id. After such an upgrade, the app is "under review" for presentation editing but not for publication. With a second `owner_publish` policy in the organization, the owner could still self-publish by naming that one.
- No seed creates two `owner_publish` policies today.
- **Fix:** decide this with box 2's `create-policy`. If versions can change kind, treat an app as under review when the newest publication's stamped kind *or* its policy's current kind is `independent_review`.

**m5. Some text still states the old limit, or ticket items that no longer apply.**
- `definition_qualification.rs:1507` still lists "the server does not yet resolve the organization's applicable policy (review m3), so an owner could choose owner_publish". This campaign now proves the opposite, and the line ships in its report.
- `evidence/APP-010/README.md:52` states the same limit. Under the evidence rules it should get a dated note in a new residuals section rather than an edit.
- The ticket's "What changes" item 1 still reads "refuses any caller-named policy id". Item 6 waits on "server-side policy resolution". Under the per-app reading the caller still names the policy and the server constrains it. Box 1's "server-resolved policy" is likewise met in substance, not literally.
- **Fix:** restate items 1 and 6 to match the ADR. This applies especially to the `policy_epoch` follow-up.

**m6. Small gaps in the campaign.**
- The check label at `composer_qualification.rs:234` still says "names the owned authoring app", while the code asserts there is none.
- The kept records-only compose (`:545-555`) never asserts `kept.app == shell`, and the reviewed `APP`'s publication count is not rechecked after the adapted and kept steps. The ADR's "keeping land[s] on the shell" therefore rests on the footprint plus the publish rule, not on a direct assertion.
- **Fix:** add `assert_eq!(kept.app, shell)` and a final count for `APP`.

### Observation outside this slice (already present before it)

`publish_once` always records the app's owner as `author_id` (`definitions.rs:1212`), and `definition_revisions` records no drafter. `admission_operator.py:169` lets the operator grant both `edit_definition` and `review_publish` per app. A non-owner who holds both can therefore draft a revision and approve it under `independent_review`, and the record names the owner as its author. Separation of duties rests on the operator never granting both to one member. This is worth a ticket. It is not graded against box 1.

## Verdict on box 1

**Met. No blocking findings.**
- The server refuses an owner who names `owner_publish` for an app under review. It does so inside the serializable, fenced publish transaction, which every publish path reaches.
- The composer no longer drafts onto such an app.
- Both cases fail on the old code: definition campaign `:1234` and composer campaign `:208`, each reproduced here.
- The ADR amendment is accurate and appended.

m1 is the one finding I would fix before landing, because it is cheap and makes "stays under review" true for databases that already went through the bypass. m2–m6 can follow, in this ticket's later slices or in box 2.
