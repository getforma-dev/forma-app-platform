### Findings
- **R1 (major, fix before the purge is relied on): cross-schema children block or escape the purge.** `genui_identity.linked_apps` and `genui_identity.share_events` reference `genui.live_work_shares(organization_id, share_id)` with non-deferrable NO ACTION foreign keys (`0017_live_sharing.sql:79,90`), and `genui_identity.shared_auth_flows` references `genui.organizations` twice and `live_work_shares` (`0017:38-40,71`). `foreign_keys()` restricts both ends to `nspname='genui'` (`admission_operator.py:1049-1054`) and `content_tables()` to `nspname='genui'` (`:1035`), so these children are invisible: if any live share of the organization was ever accepted or linked by a guest account, `DELETE FROM genui.live_work_shares` fails on the FK, the transaction rolls back (safe: nothing deleted) and the operator sees only `admission_operator_refused`; if none was, the purge succeeds but the claim "every row with an organization_id" is still false for `genui_identity`. These rows carry the share's identifiers and, in `share_events.canonical` (up to 4096 bytes), the event body; `live_work_shares` itself carries `recipient_email` and `title` and is deleted. The purge test fixture has no live share (`test_admission_operator.py`, the purge test seeds principals, memberships, a code, a box and a file), so it did not see this. Required: (a) `foreign_keys()` over every schema, so a child outside `genui` is at least ordered or named; (b) a decision on the identity rows: `share_events` is a per-account hash chain, so deleting an organization's events would break guests' chains; the honest options are to keep `live_work_shares` rows that a guest linked (with `recipient_email` and `title` blanked by an UPDATE, which the operator may do as `genui_owner`) and `linked_apps` marked `removed_at`, or to delete the identity rows under the migration-0021 precedent (the owner lifts `FORCE` on those tables inside the transaction and restores it before commit; `genui_owner` cannot otherwise read account-scoped rows, since no role has `BYPASSRLS`); (c) a test with an accepted live share and a linked guest account; (d) the ADR-007 D7.19 amendment and the runbook §10 preamble stating exactly what stays in `genui_identity`. Until then the runbook should say that a purge refusal with `live_work_shares` present means a guest-linked share and must not be forced.
- **R2 (major, externals, not W3's code): the identity provider keeps the people.** The purge deletes `principals` (issuer, tenant, subject) and `provider_invitations`, but the 25 sign-in accounts with their email addresses live in Victor's fAuth tenant at FormaStack. The notice says "This workspace's data is deleted after the test, by December 14"; either FormaStack deletes the tenant's users (or the tenant) by Dec 14 on Victor's written request, added to the Nov 23 FormaStack item and to §10, or the notice says the sign-in accounts are retained by the provider. Add to the go/no-go list.
- **R3 (minor).** `connect()` sets `statement_timeout=5000` and `lock_timeout=3000` (`admission_operator.py:661`); a single `DELETE` over 5 s aborts the whole purge (safe, nothing deleted) with the generic refusal. Say so in §10 and run it off-hours; for 25 people the counts are small.
- **R4 (minor).** The capability secret may still hold entries for redeemed or exhausted codes of the organization after the purge (only `revoke` removes an entry; `reconcile`'s sweep removes the rest, `admission_operator.py:1161,2110`). §10 should end with the operator's `reconcile` so the secret names no code of the purged organization.
- **R5 (minor).** `purge-organization` requires exactly `len(CHAINS)` audit heads (`:1096`); true for the trial organization, a refusal for any older one. Fine, but name the reason in the runbook.
