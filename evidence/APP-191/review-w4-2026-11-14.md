## Findings

### MAJOR

**M1. A personal reviewer grant (`record_review`) cannot be revoked, and a stale grant becomes live again.**
- **Where:**
  - `0024_private_entries_and_roles.sql:174-184` writes `work_permissions(record_review)`.
  - `authorization.rs:806` (`reviewer_clause`) honors any such row while the member is active.
  - `app/runtime/admission_operator.py:169-170` (`GRANTABLE`) and `:410-412` refuse `record_review` for both `grant-permission` and `revoke-permission`.
  - No definer or route deletes the row.
  - The ADR-007 amendment (`docs/architecture/ADR-007-PHASE-1-WRITE-AUTHORITY.md:184`) says "revocation of a personal grant stays with the operator". For this permission that is not true.
- **Failure scenario:**
  1. The owner gives student S the reviewer role, by mistake or for a while. The W2b dialog offers viewer, contributor and reviewer per member.
  2. Nothing can take it back. The operator refuses (`permission_refused`). A new share cannot downgrade, because `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` only adds. The only remedy is to remove S from the organization.
  3. Removing record access does not help either. Suppose the operator revokes S's `record_read` to cut S off, and later the owner shares the work with everyone as viewer.
  4. Through the organization-wide share, S passes `work_clause(record_read)`. Through the dangling row, S passes `reviewer_clause`. S again sees every member's private entries and can run `reviewer_only` commands.
- **Fix:**
  - Add `"record_review": "work"` to `GRANTABLE`, with its qualifier and operator tests. W3 owns `admission_operator.py`, so coordinate there. Revocation already advances the member's epoch.
  - Or add an owner-side definer that removes a member's role in one audited unit.
  - Correct the ADR sentence either way.
  - Until then, the share dialog should not offer reviewer to members, or the trial charter should state that removal is the only remedy.

### MINOR

**m1. The SQL definers do not fully judge what their comments claim.**
- **What changed:**
  - 0008's `share_work` refused a sharer without `record_read` (`0008_sharing.sql:126-127`).
  - 0024's replacement (`0024:140-164`) dropped that check and always inserts `record_read` for the grantee.
  - `set_work_everyone` (`0024:244-268`) also never checks the sharer's `record_read`. Its granted set always includes app `discover` (`0024:315`), although only `run` is checked (`0024:252-253`).
- **Why it matters:** The headers say each permission is "copied only when the sharer holds it" (`0024:22-23`, `:151`, `:256`). The campaign says "the SQL definers judge the same rules themselves" (`private_entries_campaign.rs:1179`). Neither is fully true.
- **Not reachable today:** The Rust precheck (`sharing.rs:273-276`, `RecordRead`) stands in front of both definers.
- **Scenario:** A future caller of either definer, or a `share_work` holder without `record_read`, would confer `record_read` and `discover` that the sharer lacks.
- **Fix:** Restore the 0008 check in both definers, as `v_creator OR record_read grant OR live org share carrying it`, and copy app `discover` into the org-wide set only when the sharer holds it. Or reword the comments and the campaign claim.

**m2. Standing that comes only from an organization-wide share can be copied into durable personal grants.**
- **Where:** `v_write` and `v_review` in both definers (`0024:153-162`, `:257-266`) count the live organization-wide share.
- **Scenario:**
  1. While an organization-wide contributor or reviewer share is live, a member with share authority (any `share_work` holder) shares personally with others.
  2. They grant `record_write` or `record_review` that exists only because of the org share.
  3. The owner then revokes the organization-wide share "as one unit". The personal copies survive, and with M1 the reviewer copies cannot be removed.
- **Reach today:** Small, because `share_work` holders other than creators are rare (code templates only; the operator cannot grant it).
- **Fix:** Exclude the organization-wide disjunct from `v_write` and `v_review` in `share_work`. Personal grants should copy personal standing only. Or record the behavior in the ADR amendment.

**m3. Concurrent organization-wide shares answer 503 instead of a result.**
- **Mechanism:** Two concurrent `set_work_everyone` calls for the same work both see no live row. The `FOR UPDATE` at `0024:271-272` locks nothing to wait on. Both then serialize on the audit head, and the second hits the partial unique index (`0024:94-95`) with 23505.
- **Result:** `Transaction::query` maps that to `Error::Unavailable` (`authorization.rs:398-406`), which `http::unavailable()` turns into 503. The invariant "never two live rows" holds; the caller simply gets an error.
- **Fix:** Take a stable lock before reading the live row (for example `SELECT … FROM genui.saved_work … FOR UPDATE` on the work), or catch 23505 and re-read. Add a two-session race check to the campaign.

**m4. The campaign does not walk every path, and the evidence claims more than it proves.**
- **The plan's rule 4:** a campaign that walks "every read and write path with a contributor, a reviewer, another member and a guest. Each path's check fails with the helper bypassed."
- **Never exercised under bypass** (removing `own_rows` from them, or swapping their app/work arguments, fails no test; only the scan test keeps the call present):
  - `cards.rs:576` `receipts_known`;
  - `record_query.rs:188` `release`;
  - `queries.rs:785`, the source-view snapshot sequence;
  - both guest sites (`shared_reads.rs:140` and guest `records_many`). The guest is exercised only as the refused live share.
- **Asserted only as an outcome:** the pinned-answer check would still pass with the `hidden_read`/`hidden_own` clause (`cards.rs:297-305`) removed.
- **D8 "even on their own entries" is not proven end to end:**
  - Ben's `approve` of his own claim (`private_entries_campaign.rs:684-698`) is also refused by `by_filer`, because `approve` is a transition. It is not run under bypass.
  - Only the unit test `reviewer_only_commands_are_refused_for_contributors` shows the gate alone.
- **No route-level run:** there is no HTTP, MCP or Slack run. I traced those callers to the tested storage functions; see "Verified as sound".
- **Fix:**
  - Add judged checks for `receipts_known` over another member's row and for `release`.
  - Add a reviewer-only `update` without transitions (for example a `grade` field) on the contributor's own row, under bypass.
  - Add a guest check that reaches `shared_reads` on a work whose shared collection sits beside an `own` one. This needs a fixture-inserted live share, since creation is refused.

**m5. The `owner_only` derivation can fail closed, and in one case open.**
- **Where:** `0024:69-70` parses `encode(canonical_schema,'escape')::jsonb`.
- **Behavior:**
  - Any non-ASCII byte or backslash aborts the insert. For a migration that means the whole 22→24 transaction.
  - An escaped spelling of `"own"` reads false, so an `own` collection would be treated as shared. Probe above.
- **Why it is not a live risk:** Unreachable today. The forma-json-v1 record-schema grammar is identifiers, enum words and integers, the Rust encoder writes them literally, and all 26 probed schemas were plain ASCII.
- **Why it still matters:** The single security switch rests on a parsing trick that a later edition (a free-text `label` on a record schema) or a non-canonical writer would break silently.
- **Fix:**
  - Add `CHECK (encode(canonical_schema,'escape') !~ '\\')` on `definition_record_schemas`. It is immutable and refuses both cases at write time.
  - Add a release preflight query on beta before release B.

**m6. The release plan still says release B is 22→23.**
- **Where:** On `main`, `TRIAL-25-READINESS.md:62` names Release B as "22→23", and `:133` makes "schema 23" the go/no-go read-back.
- **Mismatch:** With W4 merged, release B is 22→24, and the binary refuses anything but 24 (`pool.rs:607`).
- **Scenario:** A checklist that expects 23 fails on the day. Worse, a schema-release record or scan disposition prepared for 23 only blocks the release.
- **Fix:** Update the plan, `ops/promotion/schema-release.json` (release B) and the disposition to 24. These belong to W1/W3 and need Victor's approval.

**m7. The composer cannot produce the new grammar.**
- **Gap:** The plan's W4 section includes "Composer: … generate both properties from plain words … generation prompts and admission tests are added". `composer/prompt.rs:99` still describes record schemas as `{id, version: 1, fields}`, and there is no prompt or test for `visibility` or `reviewer_only`.
- **Effect:** Victor's Nov 24 plan ("builds his class apps on beta with the composer") cannot yield private entries or reviewer-only commands.
- **Not a security risk:** I checked that an existing work cannot lose `visibility` through a later publication. Adoption requires identical data content (`work_presentation.rs:132`).
- **Fix:** Ship the composer step, or record it as deferred with the plan owner.

### NIT

- **n1. Evidence counts drift.** The break-and-restore table says "17 checks" fail under bypass and "all 33 checks pass". The report has 34 checks, of which 16 are bypass-judged (`grep -c "judged!("` = 16).
- **n2. Wider share metadata reads.** `0024:103-104` grants the three application roles column SELECT on every `work_shares` row (sharer, grantee, role), though only the organization-wide rows are needed. It is metadata only, and RLS keeps it inside the organization. A view or RESTRICTIVE policy limited to `grantee_id IS NULL` would narrow it.
- **n3. Probably unused index.** `internal_record_revisions_creator` (`0024:73-74`) does not match the rule's predicate `coalesce(creator_id=…,false) OR collection_id<>ALL(…)`, so the planner will not use it. It costs write amplification and a SHARE lock during the migration.
- **n4. Record-id oracle and stray row locks.**
  - A commit naming another member's record id answers Denied (404), while a fresh id creates. That is an existence oracle on 122-bit ids.
  - Before the rule judges, `commit` takes `FOR UPDATE` on that row (`commands.rs:1225`).
  - Low risk with random ids. Document it.
- **n5. A reviewer-only commit can land just after the organization-wide reviewer share is revoked.**
  - The commit judges reviewer standing (`commands.rs:1118-1121`) without locking the share row. Personal revocations are serialized through the member epoch; the organization-wide revocation is not.
  - The window is one transaction.
  - Fix with `FOR SHARE` on the live organization-wide row inside the judgment, or document it.
- **n6. Integration notes.**
  - `Audience::Organization`, `ShareRole::Reviewer` and `revoke_organization_share` are reachable from no route yet; W2b's wiring (CSRF, the `app.shared` hint for organization-wide shares, error mapping) needs its own Tier A look.
  - `sharing.rs:377` has an empty `impl Workspace {}`.
- **n7. Non-reviewers can withdraw the owner's reviewer share.** A `share_work` holder who is not a reviewer can revoke the owner's organization-wide reviewer share or replace it with a lower role. It only reduces access, but it is not quite "the owner controls who reviews".

## Verified as sound

**Authorization: one row rule, applied everywhere data is read or written**
- `own_rows` covers every non-test record statement: the 14 statements the scan counts, plus `current_state` as a column.
  - The rule's arguments are correct at every site: the parameter order of app and work, and the `v` or `c.app`/`c.work` aliases.
  - `own_rows`, `hidden` and `reviewer_clause` are fully parenthesized and NULL-safe. A NULL principal is never a creator or reviewer, `<> ALL('{}')` is true, and `EXISTS` is never NULL.
- **Aggregates and counts.**
  - Query snapshots: both the bounds CTE and the row read carry the rule.
  - Record components: both the bounds and the rows carry it.
  - Release and the source-view sequence also carry it.
  - Listing truncation is computed after the rule. `CollectionSummary` and `RecordsListing` carry no totals, so a contributor's counts, sums and ranks cover their own rows only.
  - The only work-wide observation left is `copy_capacity`'s near-limit refusal (imported works only) and the organization-wide commit counter, which predates W4.
- **The 7 allowlist entries are safe.**
  - The two version/lock reads disclose nothing before `current_state` refuses.
  - The two inserts and the update run after the rule or create a new row; the creator is carried forward from `current_state` (`commands.rs:1297`).
  - `copy_capacity` returns only a bound.
  - The read-only probe `UPDATE` is refused by its read-only transaction.
  - Each entry matches exactly one statement.
- **Detail, commit, recovery and dry run.** `current_state` returns Denied before any value. Both `RecoveryView` constructions (`commands.rs:1157`, `:1239`) go through it, and the reused-key path is covered by the campaign.
- **Live events.** `record.committed` carries its `commit_sequence`, and the gate applies `own_rows` per subscriber (`sharing.rs:414`). The Slack approver notifier re-lists as each member, and `resolve::pending` uses the listing's filtered commands.
- **Ask, MCP, Slack, canvas, ledger and pages** reach records only through the tested listing, query, components, detail, commit and dry-run entry points. Composer context reads definitions and sources only. There is no export route, and no cross-member result cache (the revision cache is for immutable sources only).

**Authorization: who has reviewer standing, and the command gate**
- **Guests.**
  - Their principal is empty, so they are never a creator or reviewer.
  - A live share of a work with an `own` collection is refused (`shared_access.rs:393`).
  - A guest share pins publication, digest and work version, so it cannot later cover an `own` collection.
  - Adoption requires identical data content, so `visibility` and `reviewer_only` cannot change on an existing work.
- **Former and revoked members.** The fence, `rm.status` and `om.status` all require an active membership. I found no reinstatement path and no ownership or creator transfer outside tests.
- **Contributors.** They cannot reach reviewer standing through admission templates (`record_review` is excluded), the operator (excluded), `share_work` (it needs reviewer standing and share authority, and never copies `share_work`) or record copying (none exists).
- **D8 is enforced on the server.** `compose` refuses first (`commands.rs:810`). It is the only judgment, and its only callers are the commit and the dry run. Reviewer standing is re-read after the fence, and any error fails closed (`.is_ok()`). Listings, details and Slack DMs only present.

**SQL definers**
- **Definition:** Both are `SECURITY DEFINER STRICT`, with `SET search_path=pg_catalog,pg_temp`. They were created under `SET LOCAL ROLE genui_admit`, so the owner is the NOLOGIN definer role. They run `REVOKE ALL FROM PUBLIC` and grant EXECUTE to `genui_workspace` only, and `CREATE` on `genui_private` is revoked again.
- **Authority checks:**
  - The context fence on organization, principal and epoch.
  - Organization and sharer rows `FOR SHARE`.
  - Share authority as in ADR-011 (creator or `share_work`, plus personal `run`). `share_work` is never copied.
  - The reviewer role only from a reviewer; the builder's break proof shows this.
- **Audit and isolation:**
  - Audit rows in the same transaction under the head `FOR UPDATE`, with correct chain arithmetic for revoke-then-grant. Digests can be recomputed from the row.
  - Organization isolation by explicit `p_org`, RLS `WITH CHECK` and `SCOPED_ORG` in every clause. An organization-wide share cannot cross organizations.
  - FORCE RLS is consistent with the grants; the qualifier pins the exact column privileges.
- **Revocation and the one-row rule:** One call revokes as a unit. A role change writes one revocation and one grant in the same transaction. The partial unique index keeps at most one live row (see m3 for the error path).

**Migration**
- 22→24 runs in one transaction (`runtime.apply_migrations`), proving the catalog at each step. A failure in 0024 rolls 0023 back too.
- The generated column filled correctly on existing rows (probe).
- The CHECK changes validate trivially on existing rows. Existing `work_shares` rows keep `role` NULL with a non-null grantee, and every clause filters on `grantee_id IS NULL`.
- Locks are ACCESS EXCLUSIVE rewrites and scans of small tables plus one non-concurrent index build, which is acceptable inside the release window.
- **The dropped `share_events` foreign key.** Only `audit_account` (`shared_access.rs:252`) writes `share_events`, always for a share it has just read. `linked_apps` and `shared_auth_flows` keep their keys. The qualifier asserts the exact key set, and the campaign shows the purge order.
- `catalog-v24.json`, the manifest hashes and every runtime pin agree.

**Contract**
- The edition is additive: two optional members with one value each, no version bump and no new domain.
- `impl-2026-11-01.2` is retained byte-identically, and the lock is regenerated and verified.
- The eight negative vectors are reproduced in Rust with codes and paths. They cover `"shared"`, `false`, a wrong type, an unknown word and each member on the wrong shape. Older documents keep their verdicts.

**Tests**
- **Bypass hook:** `BYPASS` exists only under `#[cfg(test)]`. The non-test `bypassed()` is `false` and inlined; release, dev and integration builds cannot reach it. The campaign is `#[ignore]` and runs alone.
- **Break proofs:** Of the builder's proofs, the definer reviewer-rule and `owner_only` breaks prove what they claim, because the catalog was regenerated so the test judged the rule rather than the pin.
